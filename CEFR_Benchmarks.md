# CEFR Benchmarks — Can-Do Checklist

Based on the official Council of Europe CEFR Self-Assessment Grid (Table 2, CEFR 2001/Companion Volume 2020).
Sources: [Council of Europe self-assessment grid](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/table-2-cefr-3.3-common-reference-levels-self-assessment-grid) · [Europass grid (PDF)](https://europass.europa.eu/system/files/2020-05/CEFR%20self-assessment%20grid%20EN.pdf) · [CEFR level descriptions](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/level-descriptions)

Tick a box only with **evidence** (a drill or piece of writing that demonstrates it), and note the evidence after the box, e.g. *(Evidence: placement test, cold, [date])*.

**Status: not assessed yet.** This must always agree with the Level section of `dutch_learning_profile.md`.

Levels: A1–A2 = Basic User · B1–B2 = Independent User · C1–C2 = Proficient User

## A1 — Breakthrough

### Reading
- [ ] I can understand familiar names, words and very simple sentences, e.g. on notices, posters, catalogues.

### Writing
- [ ] I can write a short, simple postcard-style text (e.g. holiday greetings).
- [ ] I can fill in forms with personal details (name, nationality, address).

### Listening
- [ ] I can recognise familiar words and very basic phrases about myself, my family and immediate surroundings when people speak slowly and clearly.

### Speaking (interaction)
- [ ] I can interact in a simple way if the other person talks slowly and is prepared to repeat or rephrase.
- [ ] I can ask and answer simple questions about immediate needs or very familiar topics.

### Speaking (production)
- [ ] I can use simple phrases and sentences to describe where I live and people I know.

---

## A2 — Waystage

### Reading
- [ ] I can read very short, simple texts.
- [ ] I can find specific, predictable information in everyday material (advertisements, menus, timetables).
- [ ] I can understand short simple personal letters/emails.

### Writing
- [ ] I can write a series of simple phrases and sentences linked with simple connectors like "and", "but" and "because".
- [ ] I can write short, simple notes and messages relating to immediate needs.
- [ ] I can write a very simple personal letter, e.g. thanking someone.

### Listening
- [ ] I can understand phrases and high-frequency vocabulary about personal topics (family, shopping, local area, employment).
- [ ] I can catch the main point in short, clear, simple messages and announcements.

### Speaking (interaction)
- [ ] I can communicate in simple, routine tasks requiring a direct exchange of information on familiar topics.
- [ ] I can handle very short social exchanges.

### Speaking (production)
- [ ] I can use a series of phrases and sentences to describe in simple terms my family, other people, living conditions, my education and my current job.

---

## B1 — Threshold

### Reading
- [ ] I can understand texts that consist mainly of high-frequency everyday or job-related language.
- [ ] I can understand the description of events, feelings and wishes in personal letters.

### Writing
- [ ] I can write simple connected text on familiar topics or topics of personal interest.
- [ ] I can write personal letters describing experiences and impressions.

### Listening
- [ ] I can understand the main points of clear standard speech on familiar matters (work, school, leisure).
- [ ] I can understand the main point of many radio or TV programmes on current affairs or personal/professional topics when delivery is relatively slow and clear.

### Speaking (interaction)
- [ ] I can deal with most situations likely to arise while travelling where the language is spoken.
- [ ] I can enter unprepared into conversation on familiar topics (family, hobbies, work, travel, current events).

### Speaking (production)
- [ ] I can connect phrases in a simple way to describe experiences, events, dreams, hopes and ambitions.
- [ ] I can briefly give reasons and explanations for opinions and plans; narrate a story or relate a plot.

---

## B2 — Vantage

### Reading
- [ ] I can read articles and reports on contemporary problems in which writers adopt particular attitudes or viewpoints.
- [ ] I can understand contemporary literary prose.

### Writing
- [ ] I can write clear, detailed text on a wide range of subjects related to my interests.
- [ ] I can write an essay or report, passing on information or giving reasons for or against a particular point of view.

### Listening
- [ ] I can understand extended speech and lectures, and follow complex lines of argument on reasonably familiar topics.
- [ ] I can understand most TV news and current affairs programmes and the majority of films in standard dialect.

### Speaking (interaction)
- [ ] I can interact with a degree of fluency and spontaneity that makes regular interaction with native speakers quite possible.
- [ ] I can take an active part in discussion in familiar contexts, accounting for and sustaining my views.

### Speaking (production)
- [ ] I can present clear, detailed descriptions on a wide range of subjects and explain a viewpoint giving advantages and disadvantages of options.

---

## C1 — Effective Operational Proficiency

### Reading
- [ ] I can understand long and complex factual and literary texts, appreciating distinctions of style; specialised articles and longer technical instructions even outside my field.

### Writing
- [ ] I can express myself in clear, well-structured text, expressing points of view at some length; write about complex subjects selecting a style appropriate to the reader.

### Listening
- [ ] I can understand extended speech even when not clearly structured and when relationships are only implied; understand TV and films without too much effort.

### Speaking
- [ ] I can express myself fluently and spontaneously without much obvious searching for expressions; use language flexibly for social and professional purposes.
- [ ] I can present clear, detailed descriptions of complex subjects, integrating sub-themes and rounding off with an appropriate conclusion.

---

## C2 — Mastery

### Reading
- [ ] I can read with ease virtually all forms of the written language, including abstract, structurally or linguistically complex texts.

### Writing
- [ ] I can write clear, smoothly-flowing text in an appropriate style; complex letters, reports or articles with effective logical structure; summaries and reviews of professional or literary works.

### Listening
- [ ] I have no difficulty understanding any kind of spoken language, even at fast native speed, given some time to get familiar with the accent.

### Speaking
- [ ] I can take part effortlessly in any conversation or discussion, with good command of idiomatic expressions and colloquialisms; express finer shades of meaning precisely.

## Promotion rule

A level is "achieved" for a skill when ALL its boxes are ticked with evidence — and the evidence must be **COLD** (no warm-up, no reminder, ideally after a multi-day gap). A hot streak is not retention: a promotion granted on a same-day streak is typically reversed by the next cold test.

**Current status: not assessed yet.** Set after the placement test (see `CLAUDE.md` → Onboarding).

Track the skills the learner focuses on (recorded in the profile). Skills outside the focus can stay unticked; say so here.
